# Platform readiness — 6 October 2026

A verbatim record of two adversarial audits run on 6 Oct 2026, preserved because
every finding here was verified against the code or against production and would
otherwise exist only in a chat transcript.

- **Audit A** — the five-year platform question: can strangers be onboarded,
  solicited and sold to? Six dimensions plus a completeness critic (7 agents).
- **Audit B** — the January walk v5 cash-claim defects: four candidates, three
  distinct-lens skeptics each, refute-first (14 agents).

Ground truth at the time, verified live: 21 lots, ZERO households, charges,
payments, claims or renters. Payments gated off (`LAKELIFE_PAYMENTS_LIVE` unset),
no processor. 0 of 81 texts ever delivered; A2P brand approved, campaign pending.
Email verified end to end. 190 migrations, 325 test files, 7,438 tests.

`DECIDED` marks behaviour the repo chose on purpose — **not** a gap. Those are
recorded so nobody re-opens them by accident.

### By dimension, in full

#### Can a stranger park owner get started without the founder?

*Verdict: **as-expected***

> A stranger can tell you about their park and, once you have set it up for them, run it well on their own — but the first mile is eight manual steps, three of which cannot be done inside the product at all, and only one person's account can perform any of them.

- **[DECIDED · blocks Park #50 · weeks · FOUNDER-ONLY]**
  There is no self-serve park signup. /for-parks is a two-field enquiry form, and the only thing that can create a park is an ops-only action behind the Add a park button on the ops dashboard. The page says so in plain words to the visitor, and the action records the reason: creating a park hands somebody a rent ledger for nineteen households, so it is not self-serve.

  *Evidence:* src/app/for-parks/page.tsx:100-102 ("There is no instant signup, and that is on purpose"); src/app/ops/parks-actions.ts:22-25 ("OPS-ONLY, DELIBERATELY"); src/app/ops/parks-actions.ts:67-68 (assertOps gate); src/components/ops/NewPark.tsx:36 (the only door)

- **[gap · blocks Park #2 · days · FOUNDER-ONLY]**
  Creating a park REQUIRES a lake, and only lakes already on file can be chosen — yet nothing anywhere lets ops create a lake. A lake row can only be born by a homeowner typing "my lake isn't listed" in the profile wizard or a crew adding one during onboarding. So the second park you sell cannot be created at all unless it happens to sit on a lake some customer already named. The ops lake screen only edits season dates on lakes that exist.

  *Evidence:* src/app/ops/parks-actions.ts:74 (refuses with no lakeId), :91-97 (lake must exist and be non-fixture); src/lib/lake-birth.ts:50-53 and :77 (findOrCreateLake, source is only 'customer' or 'crew'); src/app/profile/actions.ts:83 and src/app/vendor/onboarding-actions.ts:393 (the only two callers); src/components/ops/LakeConditions.tsx (edits dates, creates nothing)

- **[gap · blocks Park #2 · days]**
  The park owner must already have an account before the park can be created, and nothing invites them to make one. Crews get an invite email with a claim link (ops/crews-invite.ts sends one); residents get an invite email and a printed slip. The park owner — the paying customer — has neither. Worse, createPark sends nothing: it imports no email or SMS at all, so after you create their park the owner is never told it exists. Both ends of the handoff happen in your own inbox or on the phone.

  *Evidence:* src/app/ops/parks-actions.ts:89 ("they need an account first"); src/components/ops/NewPark.tsx:94 (placeholder "they need an account already"); src/app/ops/parks-actions.ts has no sendEmail/sendSms import (verified by grep); contrast src/app/ops/crews-invite.ts:4,549 and src/lib/park-invite.ts:3-16 (residents only)

- **[gap · blocks A sale · hours]**
  An enquiry from a prospective park owner notifies nobody. The form writes a park_enquiries row; the only reader is a card on the ops dashboard. The nightly digest — the email that tells ops what needs a human — has no enquiry step. If you do not open /ops, a lead sits there silently, and the person who filled the form believes they have been heard.

  *Evidence:* src/app/for-parks/actions.ts:51 (the only writer, no email); src/app/ops/parks-data.ts:147-183 (getParkEnquiries, the only reader); src/components/ops/ParkEnquiries.tsx:50; src/app/api/cron/nightly/route.ts:112-154 (no enquiry step among the twenty)

- **[gap · blocks Park #50 · days]**
  There can only ever be one ops account, so onboarding throughput is one person — you. assertOps requires users.role = 'ops', a database trigger blocks any role change not made by ops, and the only role write anywhere in the product sets 'vendor' when a crew claims an invite. Nothing in the product can make a second ops user, so you cannot hand park setup to an employee without a hand-written SQL update against production.

  *Evidence:* src/app/ops/data.ts:18-35 (role must equal 'ops'); supabase/migrations/0002_rls.sql:56-70 (guard_role_change); src/app/ops/crews-invite.ts:790 (the only users.role writer in src, and it writes 'vendor')

- **[not built · blocks Park #50 · hours]**
  Latitude and longitude are typed in by hand. The create-park form tells ops to right-click the park entrance in Google Maps and copy the pair, and refuses to submit without them, because without a pin the park is invisible to the crew geo-gate. There is no geocoding call anywhere in src, although the address is already being collected one field above.

  *Evidence:* src/components/ops/NewPark.tsx:83-87 ("Right-click the park entrance in Google Maps and copy the pair"), :44-47 (coordsOk gate); no geocode call exists in src (verified by grep for geocode/Geocod)

- **[gap · blocks A sale · months · FOUNDER-ONLY]**
  Nothing can bill a park for using LakeLife. The $8/lot/month is ops-only, defaults to OFF for every park (feeStartMonth null is the shipping value), and raising a month writes one frozen row to lakelife_park_invoices that has no park-facing reader anywhere in the product — the status vocabulary deliberately has no 'sent' and no 'paid'. So a stranger park can be onboarded, configured and billing its own residents while LakeLife has no way to tell it what it owes or to collect it. The no-cash posture and the absent processor are deliberate; the missing invoice delivery is simply not built.

  *Evidence:* src/app/ops/park-fee-actions.ts:54-59 (both halves default to absent), :22-27 ("it does not bill, it does not email, and no park can pay it"), :119-123 (raise is ops-only); grep for lakelife_park_invoices finds readers only under src/app/ops and src/lib/park-platform-fee.ts — none under src/app/park or src/components

- **[gap · blocks — · hours]**
  Two source comments assert something that is false and has been false since before they were written: that nothing in the product can create a park. createPark landed 12 August 2026; the for-parks page and its action, written 10 September, both state there is no INSERT on parks or park_members anywhere in src. The sentence shown to a visitor is still true, so nobody is misled — but this is the repo's own worst bug class (a comment a future decision gets made on), sitting on the one file that describes how a park owner gets in.

  *Evidence:* src/app/for-parks/page.tsx:25-26 and src/app/for-parks/actions.ts:9-10 (the claim) vs src/app/ops/parks-actions.ts:116-142 (the INSERT on parks and the INSERT on park_members); git log --diff-filter=A shows parks-actions.ts at 2026-08-12 and for-parks at 2026-09-10

**What is genuinely strong here:** Everything AFTER the park row exists is genuinely self-serve and will still hold up in five years. The whole /park module gates on park_members, never on an ops role, so the owner does all of it themselves (src/app/park/import/page.tsx:14, src/app/park/setup/page.tsx:26). Three pieces stand out. (1) The readiness model: twelve rows, each one earned from the column it names, each naming the button that writes it in that button's own words, and honest about the two it cannot offer (src/app/park/readiness.ts:14-28, 310-392) — that is a working "what do I do next" for a stranger, not a demo. (2) The importer is the strongest thing in the dimension: a file door that sniffs encoding and delimiter, maps header synonyms, infers a shape when there is no header row, refuses SSNs and bank details by name, never drops a line, never invents a rent, and then creates the lots, the rates and the tenancies from one upload (src/lib/roll-parse.ts:1-24, src/app/park/import-actions.ts:647-682, src/components/ParkImportPaste.tsx:74-110). A stranger with a seller's spreadsheet types almost nothing. (3) The "build for every park, not The Haven" rule HELD on this dimension: I found no Haven value baked in as a default anywhere in src — the $542.53, the $142.53 and the 21 lots appear only inside explanatory comments, lot defaults are generic per site type (src/app/park/park-helpers.ts:507-516), and rent_due_day defaults to 1 in the schema (supabase/migrations/0059_park_import.sql:222). Also better than expected: a park owner's first sign-in is routed to /park rather than the lake-house wizard (src/app/welcome/page.tsx:30-35), and nothing in the park-owner path requires SMS verification, so the dead texting channel does not block onboarding at all.

#### What breaks operationally between 1 park and 50

*Verdict: **better-than-expected***

> The unattended machinery is genuinely built for many parks — it loops them all with real per-park isolation and no Haven constants baked in — but you cannot SEE a park you don't personally belong to, you cannot reach a second park's screens at all, and every preview deployment is wired to the live production database with the live keys.

- **[gap · blocks A sale · decision-not-work · FOUNDER-ONLY]**
  Every production secret is also a preview secret, so every PR branch deploy runs against the live database with the live service-role key. Verified on Vercel today: SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY, NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL, CRON_SECRET, GATE_ENCRYPTION_KEY, TWILIO_* and RESEND_API_KEY all carry target ["production","preview"]. Only the four RESEND_WEBHOOK_* vars are production-only. Two compounding consequences: a preview deploy can write to real households with RLS bypassed, and a preview URL's /api/cron/nightly authenticates with the same CRON_SECRET — so it would run the real nightly against real data and, because of the claim row below, silently turn that night's real 8pm run into a no-op for every park.

  *Evidence:* Vercel project lakelife env ids EMGsBHKifBEDHMlO (SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY), 1Pp4f9VYoSh1ENKw (NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL), nOw96CADiRyxErlr (CRON_SECRET), opzqXskHT4iD0EEQ (GATE_ENCRYPTION_KEY) — all target production+preview; grep -rn "VERCEL_ENV" src returns zero hits, so no code path can tell which environment it is in; src/lib/supabase/server.ts:40 reads the key unconditionally

- **[gap · blocks Park #2 · weeks]**
  When a park owner says "this number is wrong", the founder cannot see that park's money at all. There is no ops read of park_charges or park_payments anywhere in src/app/ops, and assertMyPark has no ops-role bypass — it checks park_members and nothing else. The only route to a park's ledger is to insert himself into that park's park_members, i.e. to become a manager of a customer's park in order to support it.

  *Evidence:* src/app/park/data.ts:152-176 (assertMyPark reads park_members only; no ll_is_ops branch); grep for from("park_charges")/from("park_payments") across src/app/ops/*.ts returns zero matches; src/app/ops/parks-data.ts:20-40 shows the ops park row carries occupancy counts only (lots, occupied, lapsed, vacant, pending, members) and no money

- **[not built · blocks Park #2 · weeks]**
  There is no park switcher, so the support workaround above eats itself. getMyPark returns parks[0] ordered by name across all of the user's memberships. The moment the founder is a member of two parks he can only ever reach the alphabetically-first one, and a park-management company with two parks on the platform has the same problem. The code names this as a deferred decision rather than a closed one.

  *Evidence:* src/app/park/data.ts:60-62 ("A user with more than one park gets the first by name; a park switcher is a later phase, and a silent wrong-park would be worse than a stable one") and src/app/park/data.ts:85-86 (const park = parks?.[0])

- **[gap · blocks Park #50 · days]**
  The park nightly contains one unbounded read that grows forever and will eventually kill a park's reconcile permanently. It selects EVERY charge the park has ever had with no date floor and no limit, then passes all of those ids into a single .in("charge_id", ...) — which supabase-js sends as a GET query string at roughly 37 bytes per id. There is no chunking helper anywhere in the repo. The Haven alone writes one charge per lot per month (21/month, 252/year), so the query string crosses the common 8KB request-line limit somewhere around 220 charges — inside the first year. Per-park isolation means it takes down one park's evening check at a time, every night, permanently, rather than the platform.

  *Evidence:* src/lib/park-machine.ts:175-178 (park_charges(all): .select("id, park_lot_id").eq("park_id", parkId) — no floor, no limit) and src/lib/park-machine.ts:181-189 (.in("charge_id", chargeIds.map(...))); grep -rn "chunk|batchIn|maxRows" over src/lib and src/app/park returns no chunking helper; one charge row per lot per month confirmed at src/app/park/ledger-actions.ts:494-516

- **[gap · blocks Park #50 · hours]**
  A park whose nightly fails cannot be re-run that night. The claim row is inserted before any work; on a second run the same night the unique violation returns {findings:[], error:null} — a clean, empty, successful-looking result for a park that never finished. The ok:false row is still on the screen, so the failure is visible, but there is no way to make tonight happen. At one park he waits a day; at fifty, one park's bad data means that park is dark for a day with no remedy and the re-run reports a quiet night.

  *Evidence:* src/lib/park-machine.ts:84-90 (insert claim first; if (claimErr.code === "23505") return { findings: [], error: null }) versus src/lib/park-machine.ts:264-267 (the failure path only UPDATEs the row it already claimed)

- **[gap · blocks Park #50 · hours]**
  Park-to-park isolation is enforced entirely in application code with no database backstop and no exhaustive test. Every park read uses createServiceClient(), which bypasses RLS, so the RLS policies (e.g. park_charges_read via ll_manages_park) only protect direct client queries, not the app's own ~180 guard sites. The guards are pinned one function at a time by hand-written source scanners; I found exactly one test asserting the guard string is present in a body. Nothing fails if the 46th action added in year three omits it.

  *Evidence:* src/app/park/data.ts:152-176 and every park read via createServiceClient (e.g. src/app/park/data.ts:212-216 getParkLots, commented "<- the scope"); supabase/migrations/0070_rent_ledger.sql:173-184 is the RLS the service client bypasses; the only exhaustive-style assertion is src/lib/park-properties.test.ts:85 expect(body).toContain("assertMyPark(parkId)"); 13 test files mock assertMyPark to true, acknowledged at src/app/parks/one-household-cannot-read-another.test.ts:15-17

- **[gap · blocks Park #50 · hours]**
  The ops nightly-health panel cannot say which park failed. It selects runner, run_on, ok, error and finished_at from park_machine_runs but not park_id, and then dedups the alarm by runner NAME. Since every park's runner is called "reconcile", the alarm reads "Part of the nightly run failed on 6 October (reconcile)" whether one park broke or fifty. The code already anticipates multiple parks in that very comment, so the park column was simply never added to the select.

  *Evidence:* src/app/ops/automation-health.ts:224-226 (.select("runner, run_on, ok, error, finished_at") — no park_id) and src/app/ops/automation-health.ts:149-153 (brokenRunners dedups by r.runner, comment: "with a second park there is one row per park per night, and the same runner name failing twice is one broken runner, not two")

- **[not built · blocks Park #50 · days]**
  The nightly digest's "needs a look" list has no cap and no per-park grouping. Every collected entry becomes one list item in one email. The park machine's standing finding ("N occupied lots have no bill for January 2027") is a normal, healthy output for a park with an unraised month, and it is prefixed with the park name — but at 50 parks that is 50 interleaved lines mixed in with lake-service skips in a flat list, in the one email the founder actually reads.

  *Evidence:* src/lib/digest-render.ts:249-250 (sections.failures.map to <li>, no slice or cap) fed from src/app/api/cron/nightly/route.ts:240 (for (const u of park?.urgent ?? []) failures.push(...)); the park name prefix is added at src/lib/park-machine.ts:291

### The completeness critic

#### The one thing most likely to kill this in five years

You have spent the year building the half that does not pay, and the half that pays has no door. src/app/park is 33,821 lines; src/app/book is 4,342. The park ledger is genuinely excellent — the importer, the readiness list, the append-only money trail, the per-park rate isolation are all real engineering that will still stand in five years. But your own design document (docs/park-module-design.md:40-56) says the park software is customer ACQUISITION, should be \"free or near-free\", and that the money is ~$18,400 a season of services to the park's residents. Today a resident's screen has one outbound link and it goes to /agreements. There is no path from any park surface to /book, /portal routes residents away from it permanently, and the per-lot properties row the design called for was never built. Meanwhile the thing you did decide to charge for is priced at $8/lot/month — 4x the $1-3 your own doc says the market bears — against incumbents that, for a third of the price, do the things this cannot: one-off charges and late fees, a second user, a second park, state-aware notice periods, and a data export. The five-year risk is not a bug and it is not the processor. It is that in 2031 you are selling a 4x-priced rent ledger with a thinner feature set than Rent Manager, to parks you cannot onboard without typing their coordinates by hand, while the service marketplace that was supposed to be the business has three fixture crews and no resident who can reach it. Before another migration: build the resident→service door and put a real crew behind it at The Haven in spring 2027. One park's residents actually buying one service is worth more than the next fifty ledger features, because it is the only thing that tests whether the thesis is true.

**Why that is uncomfortable to say:**

Because the honest recommendation is to stop improving the strongest code in the repository. Every instinct in this project — and the quality of the park module is evidence of a very good instinct — says to keep hardening the thing that works. The six audits all point inward at the ledger, so acting on them means more park code. But the park ledger was never the product in your own written plan; it was the on-ramp. Saying so means accepting that the months since August went into the on-ramp while the road stayed unbuilt, and that the $8/lot decision quietly turned the on-ramp into the product without anyone re-reading the analysis that said not to. It is also uncomfortable because it is unfalsifiable from inside the codebase: no test, no audit and no migration can tell you whether a mobile-home resident in Indiana will buy a winterization through an app. Only a real crew, a real resident and a real charge can, and all three are still hypothetical on 6 October 2026.
