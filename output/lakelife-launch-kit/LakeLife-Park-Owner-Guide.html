01 / The everyday benefit

Know what is owed.
See what needs doing.

LakeLife brings the records your park uses every day into one place. Your office can review bills and recorded payments, explain shared costs and keep track of property work.

A clearer money record

See scheduled charges by lot and the payments recorded against them. When a resident reports a cash or check payment, the office reviews and confirms it.

Shared costs with context

Keep the source note and allocation record alongside shared expenses such as water, sewer, trash or grounds care. Make the explanation easier to find when someone asks.

Work with evidence

Keep booked services and completion photos in the work record. A job must meet its photo requirement before it counts as complete.

A clearer resident view

Residents can see their own bill, recorded payments and receipts, and follow the status of reported issues. The office remains available to help.

A useful first conversation

Bring one billing question and one property task from your park. We will walk through how the record works and what setup would involve.

02 / A record you can explain

Follow the amount
from source to lot.

When someone asks about a balance, the answer should be a clear record. Here is a simple illustration of the conversation LakeLife supports.

Illustrative exampleAmount / action
Park expenseA $300 shared water bill, with its source note recorded.
Allocation in this example20 equally participating lots × $15 each. The actual allocation follows your park’s configured rules.
Example lot’s charges$500 rent + $15 shared cost = $515 billed.
Confirmed payment$100 paid to the office and confirmed in the record.
Remaining balance$415. A newly reported payment stays subject to office review.

This is an example, not a resident statement. Your park determines its charges and applicable allocation rules. Opening balances and cost settings are reviewed with your office before residents are invited.

For the office

Find the lot, the charge and the payment record together. Use the same facts when following up or correcting a mistake.

For the resident

See a breakdown of their own bill and ask a specific question. A reported payment and a confirmed payment should be understood as different steps.

Online payment options depend on the park’s enabled setup. If they are unavailable, residents continue using the payment method your office specifies.

03 / Property work

Keep the request
and the result together.

1 / Identify the work

Record the task and property details. For resident issues, use an installed LakeLife reporting sticker where available, or have the office log the report.

2 / Agree on the next step

Review scope, availability and whether the service has a price or needs a crew quote. A request is not a confirmed appointment.

3 / Coordinate the crew

The assigned crew receives the job details it needs. Service coverage and timing depend on local crew availability and seasonal conditions.

4 / Keep the proof

The job requires completion photos before it can count as complete. Keep the work record available for later questions.

Care for the park and the household

LakeLife’s service categories include seasonal home care, piers and lifts, boat care, lawn care and housekeeping. Confirm which services are available for your property during the walkthrough.

Optional resident services stay separate. A household’s optional service purchase is separate from the rent bill. The office can see the crew visit, service and timing; it does not see what the renter paid for that service.

Resident control

Optional services are a separate choice. Text-message preferences are handled in the account. The resident’s account is tied to their own lot record.

A helpful launch

Use the supplied Renter Welcome Guide. Tell residents who to contact, how to view their bill and how to report a concern before asking them to do more.

LakeLife helps organize records and follow-up. It does not set your park’s rent, promise a lower bill or guarantee a repair deadline. Your office remains responsible for its decisions and resident communication.

06 / Questions and a next step

Decide with the
full picture.

What does LakeLife cost?

Your written proposal shows the per-lot fee, eligible lot count, billing start and included support. It also identifies any separate processing or service charges. Review those terms before choosing a start.

Do we have to change how everyone pays?

Start by reviewing your current process. LakeLife can keep offline payment records. Online rent requires an enabled and verified park setup; use only the methods actually available to your park.

Will this replace all our accounting or reservation software?

The walkthrough starts with the workflow you need. Review any required accounting, reservation or export needs before deciding what LakeLife should handle.

What if a resident needs help?

Your office can provide the account instructions and help review the record. Use the welcome guide and confirm the actual support contact and hours during setup.
