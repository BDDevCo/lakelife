One audience.

Proposed launch window: 19 October 2026 through 16 January 2027. Use fall and winter to build owner relationships, support a careful pilot and prepare spring service demand.

The owner offer

Review one billing period and one property task. Show how the owner, office and renter each see the record. Then agree on the first setup step and commercial terms.

Before a campaign runs

Verify the destination page, inquiry delivery, named follow-up owner, supported workflow and available crew capacity. Public posts should invite a conversation that the team can handle.

Seasonal discipline

Use “ask about availability” for late-season work. Water work follows the actual lake conditions and service deadline; do not advertise a universal ice-out or pull date.

Budget assumption: the operating baseline is a lean $500 per month. A separate $1,500 option is included. Neither option authorizes spending or establishes an approved budget.

02 / Sequence

Build proof in four phases.

Days 1-14

Prepare a working demo, approved offer wording, owner checklist and resident guide. Verify a first prospect list and begin founder-led discovery. Proposed target: 10 qualified owner conversations and 4 walkthrough bookings.

Days 15-45

Complete walkthroughs, document objections and propose a small supported start. Reconcile records before resident invitations. Proposed target: 2 pilot agreements with named office contacts.

Days 46-75

Observe a complete workflow, support residents and collect permission to use any real feedback. Measure setup time and support load. Proposed target: 1 reviewed billing cycle and an evidence-based story.

Days 76-90

Review continuation at clear terms. Ask satisfied owners for an introduction they are comfortable making. Build a spring service-interest list with consent. Proposed target: 2 activated parks and documented next steps.

A pilot has a finish line

Write down the starting scope, first billing period, who checks records, how residents get help and when the owner will review the result. Keep any special price, end date and continuation terms explicit.

Use process proof until customer proof exists: show the actual workflow with synthetic data and label it “Example.” A testimonial needs the person’s permission and their real words.

Paid-test gate: start only after the inquiry route has been tested and the team can follow up. Run one message and one audience at a time; compare qualified inquiries rather than cheap clicks.

Stop or revise

Proposed rule: review after $50 spent or 7 days, whichever comes first. If there are no qualified inquiries, inspect targeting, message and destination before adding spend. This is a learning threshold, not a statistical guarantee.

Keep cost honest

Record founder sales hours separately from ad spend. Paid leads are not the whole acquisition cost. Add printing, travel, setup and support when evaluating the economics.

Placement prices and ad costs are unquoted planning allocations. This plan does not claim a particular reach, cost per lead or number of customers.

05 / Measurement

Count progress that matters.

90-day proposed funnelTargetDefinition
Verified owner contacts60Relevant park and correct business contact; no private resident lists.
Qualified conversations20Relevant need, decision role, workable location and next-step interest.
Completed walkthroughs10Owner/office sees its chosen workflow and asks fit questions.
Pilot agreements4Scope, responsibilities, price and review date are explicit.
Activated parks2Approved opening record, office workflow working and first household access verified.

This implies 33% contact-to-conversation, 50% conversation-to-walkthrough, 40% walkthrough-to-agreement and 50% agreement-to-activation. These are planning hurdles to test, not predicted conversion rates.

Spend per activation

If the lean plan spends $1,500 and activates 2 parks, marketing spend is $750 per activation. At $240 monthly fees for a 30-lot park, fee-only payback is at least 3.1 months before support and other costs. It is not profit payback.

Separate household funnel

Track service inquiries, quoted jobs, bookings, completed jobs, rework and repeat bookings separately. Do not count renters invited by a park as acquired service customers.

Attribution

Log source plus a simple campaign name, such as park-owner-fall-01. Record self-reported referral source as well. Test any tagged URL before posting it.

Do not publish these targets as LakeLife traction. Review both conversion and the reasons people declined; a smaller satisfied cohort is useful evidence.

06 / Outreach copy

Make the first message
easy to understand.

Owner introduction / email or contact form

Subject: A clearer record for your park

Copy to use

Hi, I’m Brendon with LakeLife. We help park owners bring rent records, shared costs and property work into one place, with a clearer view for the office and residents.

I’d like to walk through one billing period and one property task with you so you can see whether it fits how your park runs. You can learn more at LakeLife.ai/for-parks.

Would a short walkthrough be useful?

Follow-up after a walkthrough

Thanks for showing me how your park handles billing and property work. The next step is to confirm the lots, opening balances and first workflow we discussed. I’ll put the setup responsibilities, price and review date in a simple written scope so you can decide with the full picture.

Association introduction request

I’m building LakeLife for local property coordination around Big Long, Pretty and Big Turkey Lakes. Is there an appropriate way to introduce it to members or local park owners through your public newsletter, directory or events? I can provide a short description for your review.

Use personally and with context. Add the real property or conversation detail before sending. Respect a request not to follow up. These are prepared drafts; no outreach has been sent.

07 / Weekly operating rhythm

Keep the loop short.

Monday / 30 minutes

Review open conversations, onboarding blockers and available crew capacity. Pick the week’s one owner message and one household help topic.

Tuesday / outreach

Make a small number of relevant owner introductions. Follow up on promised next steps. Log the question that stopped each conversation.

Wednesday / demonstrate

Run walkthroughs, test a resident instruction and publish the scheduled education post. Use synthetic data for demonstrations.

Thursday / support

Help the pilot office complete its chosen workflow. Check failed account access, confusing bills and unresolved work with the responsible person.

Friday / 30 minutes

Record spend, qualified inquiries, walkthroughs, activations, support hours and service contribution. Continue, revise or stop each campaign based on evidence.

What the next 90 days should inherit

A verified prospect list, a working demonstration, a clear offer, actual setup time, resident questions, measured service contribution and customer feedback used with permission.

Execution basis: current LakeLife product materials and the companion Audience & Market Study. Timing, budgets, targets and staff rhythms are recommendations for this pilot, not existing commitments. Use the supplied calendar and tracking CSVs as the working record.
