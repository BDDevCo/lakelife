01 / Brand and message

Sound like someone
who keeps track.

Warm, local and specific. Make each post useful on its own. Lead with the person’s property or question, then explain one benefit and one next step.

PillarUseful subjectsProof to show
Owner clarityRent records, shared costs, office workflows.A real workflow with synthetic or permission-cleared data.
Resident confidenceBill breakdown, receipts, where to ask, reported issues.Plain instructions for participating parks.
Visible workService scope, completion photos, approved changes.Original service artwork or permitted real job evidence.
Seasonal readinessPlanning opening/closing, piers, lifts and home care.Actual local availability and lake conditions.

Use

“See the price before anything is charged.”
“Ask your office to review the record.”
“See whether it fits your park.”

Avoid

“Guaranteed savings.”
“Every park can collect rent online.”
“No more work for your office.”

Do not show resident names, balances, addresses, access codes or payment details in public screenshots. Label demonstrations “Example”; use real customer quotes and job photos only with permission.

02 / Channel roles

Keep the weekly rhythm
manageable.

Facebook

Primary local education and owner conversation channel. Use a business page; ask group administrators before posting in community groups. A direct relevant question is more useful than repeated promotion.

Instagram

Show the brand, service illustrations, short walkthroughs and permitted work evidence. Use captions and readable on-screen text so video still makes sense without sound.

Stories

Two short updates a week: one helpful explanation and one invitation. Repeat the most useful guide step. Use a website link only where the account supports it.

Short video

One useful demonstration every other week. A calm 20-30 second explanation can answer a question better than a feature list.

Local newsletters

Offer a short owner or homeowner paragraph to association editors. Follow their placement rules and obtain permission; do not imply endorsement.

Suggested starting cadence

Three feed posts per week for four weeks, two stories per week, and two short videos across the month. Reduce frequency if support or production quality suffers.

CTA routing: park owners → LakeLife.ai/for-parks; homeowners → LakeLife.ai; current renters → their participating park office and office-issued account instructions. Do not publicly post a resident claim code.

The supplied 1080 × 1080 graphics are a simple reusable square format, not a claim about every channel’s current preferred dimensions. Preview each platform crop before posting.

09 / Replies and questions

Answer the question.
Make the next step clear.

“How much is it?”

For owners: “The park fee depends on the agreed setup and eligible lots. A walkthrough gives us what we need to put the price and scope in writing.” For homeowners: “Review the pricing status and availability. Some requests await a crew quote; see the price before anything is charged.”

“Can I pay rent?”

“That depends on your park’s setup. Your account shows the options available to you. If online payment is not enabled, keep paying the office using its stated method.”

“Is this for my park?”

“LakeLife starts with a park-owner walkthrough. Your office can tell you whether your park is participating and how to connect to your own account.”

“My bill is wrong.”

“Please ask your park office to review your account with you. Avoid posting your address, balance or payment details here.” Move account-specific help to the actual support route.

“When can a crew come?”

“Availability depends on the service, property and crew schedule. Visit LakeLife.ai to discuss the job. A date is confirmed only when it is scheduled.”

“How do I report a repair?”

“Use your park’s installed LakeLife reporting sticker if one is available, or ask the office to log the issue. For an urgent hazard, use the park’s urgent contact procedure.”

Reply only with verified account-independent facts in public. Do not discuss a resident’s balance, tenancy or payment status in comments.

10 / Production and measurement

A repeatable hour
each week.

StepAction
15 min / choosePick the week’s three posts. Verify service readiness and the link. Choose the audience before adapting the words.
20 min / prepareUse supplied artwork; keep original colors, fonts and wave motif. Add alt text from the caption file. Preview the crop on a phone.
10 min / checkRead every claim, date and CTA. Confirm consent for photos or quotes. Have one person check spelling and readability.
15 min / reviewLog qualified inquiries, walkthrough bookings and questions. Note what confused people. Set next week’s message from those questions.

Measure

  • Qualified inquiries by post and audience.
  • Owner walkthroughs booked and completed.
  • Useful renter questions resolved by the office.
  • Service inquiries that become completed jobs.

Learn

  • Which problem gets a real response?
  • Does the CTA reach the right place?
  • Which message attracts the wrong audience?
  • Does the team have capacity to follow up?

Reach, likes and saves can help diagnose distribution, but they do not prove adoption. Keep owner and homeowner results separate, and avoid treating renter help questions as sales leads.

Use social-calendar.csv to prepare posts and campaign-tracker.csv to log results. The first four weeks are drafted; later weeks should reflect what actual conversations teach.

11 / Supplied artwork

Six graphics,
ready for your next post.

01 / Park owner

02 / Renter clarity

Format

Six 1080 × 1080 PNG images. Reuse the original brand mark, wave artwork and service illustrations. Editable compositions are supplied in social/social-artwork.html.

How to post

Pair the image with its full caption and alt text from social-calendar.csv. Keep supporting details in the caption rather than shrinking them into the graphic.

How to adapt

Change the headline and CTA within the same layout. Keep deep water blue, teal, gold, Bricolage Grotesque and Manrope. Review long copy for overflow.

Artwork is original LakeLife prototype artwork reused in branded compositions. The posts describe workflows and invitations; no customer photos, invented testimonials or unverified performance statistics are included.
