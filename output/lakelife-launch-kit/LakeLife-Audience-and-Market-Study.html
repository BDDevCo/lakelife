01 / The local starting point

Three lakes.
Two county contexts.

Begin with Big Long, Pretty and Big Turkey Lakes in Indiana. Big Long and Pretty are in LaGrange County; Big Turkey crosses LaGrange and Steuben Counties. Build the actual prospect map around crew travel and property fit.

¹ Income is stated in 2024 dollars. These are county-wide estimates; they do not describe lakefront households, seasonal owners or park renters specifically.

What this tells us

There is a broader regional household base, but the 26,955 combined households are not LakeLife’s addressable market. Lakefront counts, eligible park lots, willingness to pay and serviceable crew capacity still need to be measured.

Practical inference: offer both self-service and office-assisted onboarding. County broadband differences justify testing access options; they do not establish any individual resident’s ability or preference.

02 / Priority audiences

Choose by need,
not by stereotype.

Priority / roleJob they need doneTrigger / first proof
1 / Independent park ownerKnow what is owed and what work remains, with a record the office can explain.A disputed balance, a shared expense, seasonal turnover or scattered work notes. Show one lot and one completed task.
2 / Park manager or office leadKeep day-to-day records accurate without increasing repetitive questions.A billing run, payment reconciliation or repeated maintenance follow-up. Let them run the workflow themselves.
3 / Renter in a participating parkUnderstand their own bill and know what happened to a reported issue.A new bill, a receipt question or a maintenance concern. Show their own account and the office contact route.
4 / Lake homeownerArrange a specific service and see the price and completion proof.Seasonal closing/opening, a missed service or a trip arriving soon. Start with one available job.
Enabler / Local crewReceive clear job details and complete work with a reliable record.Open route capacity and suitable work. Confirm scope, access, photos and actual payment terms.

These profiles are hypotheses based on the product’s workflows, not findings from interviews. Validate the need, decision process and language with real people before narrowing the message further.

Buyer and user differ: the owner approves the park agreement; the office runs much of the process; renters experience the result. A strong owner sale includes a resident-friendly launch plan.

03 / The decision journey

Remove one uncertainty
at a time.

“What is it?”

Use a single sentence: “LakeLife brings park rent records, shared costs and property work into one place.” Avoid listing the whole platform before establishing the need.

“Does it fit us?”

Ask how they handle a recent bill or task. Use their workflow in the walkthrough, including cash/check records and the office’s existing practices.

“Will setup be hard?”

Show the data checklist, who reviews it and what happens before residents are invited. Agree on a scope; do not promise a fixed migration time.

“What will it cost?”

Explain the final park fee and any separate processing or service charges in the written proposal. Keep household services separate from rent.

“Will residents use it?”

Demonstrate one account from a resident’s view. Include printed instructions and office assistance. Track actual access, not just invitations sent.

“Can I trust it?”

Show the opening-balance review, payment status language, completed-work evidence and the person who handles questions.

The closing question

“If this handled the billing and work record we just reviewed, what would you need to see before starting with your park?”

Test these objections in discovery. Do not present them as quotes from customers or claim the walkthrough has already resolved them.

06 / Size from the ground up

Count what can be served.

A useful launch market is a verified list of properties and people with a recurring need. It is not a percentage of county population.

Inventory to buildFields to verifyWhy it matters
ParksLocation, decision-maker, recurring lots, eligible software lots, existing tools, next billing cycle.Creates a park-fee opportunity range and a realistic discovery list.
HouseholdsProperty location, service need, season, current provider, permission to follow up.Connects demand to a specific service rather than a generic audience.
CrewsCoverage, available days, services, insurance, unit costs and job limits.Sets the serviceable market and route capacity.
Adoption evidenceQualified conversation, walkthrough, agreement, activated account and completed service.Distinguishes interest from actual use.

Bottom-up model

Annual park fee opportunity = verified eligible lots × agreed monthly rate × paid months.

Annual household service spend = verified reachable households × likely booked jobs per year × average job total. Apply conversion and capacity limits before forecasting.

Illustration only: 100 eligible lots at $8 for 12 months = $9,600 in park fees. Separately, 40 households × 3 jobs × $300 = $36,000 in service spend; a 30% target gross spread is $10,800 before processing, rework and overhead. These inputs are not observed local counts.

Avoid adding all county households, seasonal sites and park households together: they may overlap. Maintain separate unique records, and deduplicate before estimating demand.

07 / Validate before scaling

Twelve conversations
can change this plan.

Proposed interview sampleQuestions to askEvidence to capture
5 park owners / office leadsWalk me through the last billing issue or unfinished task. What do you use now? What would make switching worthwhile?A specific recent example; current tools; decision-maker; setup concerns; willingness to review a scoped offer.
4 residents, with park permissionHow do you check a bill or ask about a repair? What would make access easier?Actual steps, confusing wording and preferred assistance; no unnecessary financial details.
3 lake homeownersWhat was the last service you arranged? How did you select a provider and know it was finished?Service frequency, timing, proof expectations and reaction to a real scoped quote.

Two simple tests

Owner message

Test “Your park, in one place” against “A clearer record of what is owed and what needs doing.” Use similar outreach groups; compare qualified walkthroughs, not likes.

Resident onboarding

Observe someone using the printed guide and someone receiving office help. Record completion, questions and incorrect account links; stop and correct access errors.

Decision rule, proposed: continue the pilot when owners identify a repeated problem, can complete the chosen workflow and agree to a specific next step at disclosed terms. Revise the offer if interest does not survive the walkthrough.

Study method: desk research from official pages plus current project review, completed 17 October 2026. No interviews or surveys have yet been conducted. Sample sizes above are a proposed qualitative learning plan; they will not produce statistically representative market estimates.
