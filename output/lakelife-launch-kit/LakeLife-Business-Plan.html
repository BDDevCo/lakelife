01 / The business

A clearer record.
A local service network.

LakeLife coordinates property work and the records around it. Start with park owners near Big Long, Pretty and Big Turkey Lakes, then serve the households and lake homeowners within a workable local crew route.

Park owners

Scheduled bills by lot, shared cost records, reported issues and crew work in one place. Guided setup is the first sale.

Renters

Understand the bill, see recorded payments and receipts, and follow reported issues. Optional household services remain separate from rent.

Homeowners

Coordinate seasonal care, piers and lifts, boat care, lawn care and housekeeping. Review one service total and see completion photos.

The first offer

“Let’s walk through one billing period and one property task at your park.” Show the owner their next useful step, then agree on a small, supported start.

Planning horizon: the first 90 days build a repeatable local pilot; the 12-month model tests whether recurring park fees and completed service work can support the operation. This is a founder planning document, not a statement of traction.

02 / Focus and positioning

Win the first useful job.

Lead with a problem the owner can point to today: a payment that needs confirming, a shared bill that needs explaining, or property work that needs a completion record.

Initial park fit

  • An owner or manager can make the buying decision.
  • Recurring lots or seasonal residents create a repeated billing and work routine.
  • The owner will reconcile opening balances and support resident onboarding.
  • The location fits the founding-lake service area and available crews.

Initial household fit

  • A lake homeowner has a defined service need.
  • A renter at a participating park wants optional care.
  • The property details are sufficient to quote the work.
  • The service can be delivered safely within a confirmed route and season.

Scope boundary: avoid leading with overnight campground reservations, full accounting replacement or a new tenant-management system. Validate those needs in discovery before proposing a fit.

Working positioning: local property coordination with clear records and visible work. Broad property software is a real alternative. LakeLife must earn the sale through a useful local workflow and responsive setup, rather than claiming to be the only option.

Competitive context: Rent Manager manufactured housing; Buildium pricing and features; Campspot pricing and features. Official pages reviewed 17 October 2026; not hands-on product tests.

04 / How the business earns

Two revenue streams.
One clear customer total.

Park software fees and service coordination fees are separate. Resident rent belongs to the park owner and is excluded from LakeLife revenue in this planning model.

Park software: $8 per eligible lot per month is an internal planning input drawn from the configured policy history, not a universal customer offer. Confirm the current setting, lot eligibility, park agreement and billing start. The code’s safe fallback is $0.

Service pricing: crew-priced work uses the crew’s quote and two configurable fee settings. Existing menu-priced work and negotiated park rates retain their own rules. Never force all work into one margin formula.

05 / Operating design

A small, accountable loop.

Qualify & scopeFounder / salesConfirm park fit, service location, decision-maker and the first workflow.

Review & resolveOperations + customerObtain homeowner approval before profile corrections change pricing; resolve missing work before completion.
Reconcile & learnFounder + bookkeepingMatch money records to actual transactions, review exceptions and record the next operating improvement.

Capacity before promotion

Keep a weekly view of open requests, crew availability, drive time, weather limits and unresolved work. Stop promoting a service whose backlog cannot be served.

Support that can be kept

Name the real support contact and operating hours during setup. Track response time in the pilot before publishing a service-level promise.

Payment gate: a bill or an invoice record is not a collected payment. Confirm the actual settlement, refund and payout process before modeling collected cash as available.

First prove the workflow.
Then repeat it.

WhenActionProposed learning target
Days 1-14Verify demo, payment wording and service capacity. Build a verified owner prospect list.10 qualified local owner conversations and 4 booked walkthroughs.
Days 15-45Walk through a real billing cycle; agree on a limited supported pilot.2 pilot agreements; each has a named office contact and reconciled starting records.
Days 46-75Observe resident access and completed work; fix recurring confusion.At least 1 park completes a reviewed billing cycle; record support time and issue resolution.
Days 76-90Ask for continuation and, with permission, a customer introduction.2 parks choose a next step; documented reasons from every decline.

These are proposed targets, not existing traction. A park is activated only when its opening records are approved, the office can complete the chosen workflow and at least one invited household can access its own record. “Paid” and “collected” remain separate statuses.

Channels to start

Founder outreach, permission-based association introductions, office handouts and local Facebook/Instagram education. Use paid promotion only after the inquiry route and follow-up are working.

Reasons to pause

Unreconciled billing, failed account access, insufficient crew capacity, unclear pricing or payment uncertainty. Keep discovery conversations open while fixing the specific issue.

Suggested channel evidence: Big Long Lake Association membership and community channels. No partnership or endorsement is implied. See the Marketing Rollout for the weekly execution plan.

Founder decisions before broad promotion

  • Confirm the park’s final fee, included support, billing start and cancellation terms in writing.
  • Choose a monthly marketing cap. This kit includes $500 and $1,500 options; its financial baseline uses $500.
  • Confirm the real support contact, operating hours, crew capacity and live payment capabilities.
  • Set a minimum acceptable contribution and a maximum setup/support workload per park.
