LakeLife / Brand foundation01
What we stand for

Less to chase.
More clarity.

LakeLife brings the details of property care into one place, so people can see what needs doing, what it costs and what happened.

The ten-second introduction

LakeLife helps park owners manage the money record and property work, and helps homeowners coordinate care for their lake homes.

The feeling

Calm, capable and local. Like someone who knows the lake and keeps track of the details.

Warm enough to feel personal. Specific enough to earn trust.

The proof

A clear service total. A record of the work. Photos before a job counts as complete. Homeowner approval for proposed changes that affect their bill.

Make the next decision easy. Explain who LakeLife helps, the problem it solves and the next step. A useful conversation is a stronger invitation than a large promise.

LakeLife / Identity03
The logo

A sun above the water

Use the original mark and wordmark from the prototype. Keep the gold sun, both wave shapes and the LakeLife lettering together.

LakeLife
Primary treatment

White “Lake” and gold “Life” on deep water blue. On light pages, place this lockup on a clean dark band.

Clear space

Leave at least one sun diameter around the whole lockup. Keep captions, borders and other marks outside that space.

The standalone mark works where the LakeLife name is already visible.

Recommended size

Full lockup: at least 120 px wide on screen or 32 mm in print. Mark alone: at least 24 px or 6 mm. Increase size if details lose clarity.

Keep it consistent

Preserve proportions. Use supplied artwork. Keep the mark upright and the wordmark on one line. Avoid shadows, outlines or extra symbols.

Layout

Keep generous margins and a clear reading order. Align text to a shared left edge. Use 14 px corner radii for cards and 10 px for action buttons when echoing the product.

LakeLife / Voice07
How LakeLife sounds

Helpful. Specific. Human.

Talk about the person’s property and the work they need. Use everyday words and tell them what happens next.

Instead of
Use

“An end-to-end property management ecosystem.”

“Rent records, shared costs and crew work in one place.”

“Unlock seamless service orchestration.”

“Choose the service you need. Review the total before you book.”

“Your request has been processed.”

“We received your request. Your date is not confirmed yet.”

“Set it and forget it forever.”

“Review the next seasonal visit and book when you’re ready.”

“Guaranteed savings with zero hassle.”

“See the price and the work record in one place.”

Use the customer’s words: crew, service, total, photos, your park, your lake home. Save terms such as routing, vendor margin and payment rails for internal conversations.

Write “scheduled” only when a date is confirmed. Write “complete” only after the job meets its completion requirements. Keep reassurance tied to a specific action.

LakeLife / Homeowner message08
Copy to use

Your lake house,
ready when you are

Lead with the next service they already need. Explain the price and the proof before describing the rest of the platform.

Short homeowner invitation

Opening the house, pulling the pier, getting the lawn ready? LakeLife brings your lake-home services into one place. Review one clear service price, keep track of the work and see completion photos in your property record.

Visit LakeLife.ai to explore services for your property.

What can LakeLife help with?

Seasonal opening and closing, piers and lifts, boat winterization and storage, lawn care and housekeeping. Confirm service availability for the property.

What will it cost?

The service and property details determine the total. Review the all-in service price before booking. Keep internal crew costs out of customer materials.

How will I know the work is done?

The job requires completion photos. The property record keeps the work and photos together.

What if the crew finds something different?

The homeowner approves proposed profile corrections before they change the profile or affect billing.

Local first: name Big Long, Pretty and Big Turkey Lakes, Indiana. Confirm coverage and seasonal conditions before promising a date.

LakeLife / Park owner message09
Copy to use

Your park,
in one place

Start with the records and work the owner already handles. Make the first step a walkthrough of their own needs.

Short park owner invitation

Running a mobile-home or RV park means keeping up with rent, shared expenses and the crews who do the work. LakeLife brings those records together, with scheduled bills by lot, shared cost records and photos before crew work counts as complete.

Visit LakeLife.ai and tell us about your park. We’ll walk through what setup would look like for you.

Do I have to set everything up myself?

Park setup is guided. Review the lots, rents and shared costs together before billing begins.

Can I still record cash and checks?

Yes. The resident records that they paid and the owner confirms receipt. Both sides are part of crediting the bill.

Can we use our existing crew?

Ask about inviting them through crew onboarding. Required documents and insurance still apply before work can be routed.

What does LakeLife cost the park?

Review the applicable setup and platform fees during the walkthrough. Quote current approved terms before the owner commits.

LakeLife Brand Guide10 / 12
LakeLife / Trust10
Promises people can understand

Be clear about
what happens

The easiest promise to believe is one the customer can see in the product.

One service total

Say what the service includes and show the customer’s total. Keep internal costing and margins out of shared sales materials.

Photos required

Say “photos before the job counts as complete.” Avoid implying a photo alone guarantees quality, condition or a customer’s satisfaction.

Approval first

For proposed profile corrections, explain that the homeowner approves before the change affects the profile or bill.

Dates with context

Separate a requested date from a confirmed visit. Lake conditions and crew availability affect seasonal work.

Payments

Describe cash and check records accurately. Confirm live payment processing before advertising automatic charges, bank debit or card collection.

Local coverage

Use confirmed service areas. Ask about the property or park before committing to a crew or start date.

Use evidence for stronger claims. Publish savings, response times, testimonials and customer counts only when verified. Offer terms such as free trials or guaranteed dates need an approved, specific offer.
